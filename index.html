Solar siblings & birth place

No solar sibling is confirmed. Candidates are stars with near-solar chemistry and/or orbits that could share the Sun’s birth cluster; later work often weakens or rejects them.

Candidates shown

  • HD 162826 (~33 pc, Hercules) — proposed by Ramírez et al. (2014); Martínez-Barbosa et al. (2016) find a low dynamical probability.
  • HD 186302 (~57 pc, Pavo) — AMBRE solar twin / sibling candidate (Adibekyan et al. 2018); Webb et al. (2020) find its actions make a common origin unlikely without a strong GMC kick.
  • Solar Sibling 1 (2MASS J19354742+4803549 / Kepler-1974) — top dynamical+chemical candidate in Webb et al. (2020) (~0.36 kpc); a 2022 association age (~40 Myr) argues against a 4.6 Gyr sibling (see overview on Solar sibling).

Dispersed birth cluster

N-body work finds the birth association/cluster has almost certainly dissolved and is spread through the disc—not a surviving open cluster like M67 (Webb et al. 2020; Portegies Zwart 2009; Martínez-Barbosa et al. 2016). The translucent ring marks a schematic present-day radial band for siblings on Sun-like orbits after bar/spiral/GMC scattering (~5–11 kpc), not a precise census.

Sun’s birth radius

Radial migration: many chemical/dynamical estimates place birth near ~5 kpc (now ~8.2 kpc). Ratcliffe et al. (2024) give 4.5±0.4 kpc; Baba et al. (2024) discuss migration from ≈5 kpc; an A&A (2025) GAM analysis finds ~7.1 kpc—so the marker uses ~5 kpc with that literature range noted.
