Solar siblings & birth place

No solar sibling is confirmed. Candidates are stars with near-solar chemistry and/or orbits that could share the Sun’s birth cluster; later work often weakens or rejects them.

Candidates shown

  • HD 162826 (~33.3 pc, Hercules; Gaia DR3 ϖ=30.068 mas) — proposed by Ramírez et al. (2014); Martínez-Barbosa et al. (2016) find a low dynamical probability.
  • HD 186302 (~56.7 pc, Pavo; Gaia DR3 ϖ=17.6309 mas) — AMBRE solar twin / sibling candidate (Adibekyan et al. 2018); Webb et al. (2020) find its actions make a common origin unlikely without a strong GMC kick.
  • Solar Sibling 1 (2MASS J19354742+4803549 / Kepler-1974) — top dynamical+chemical candidate in Webb et al. (2020). Distance here uses Gaia DR3 parallax ϖ=3.7853 mas → ~264 pc; Webb+2020 used ~0.36 kpc from DR2. Age tension (~40 Myr assoc.).

Sun’s motion

  • Galactic orbit — roughly circular at ~220–240 km/s toward galactic longitude ℓ≈90°, b≈0 (direction of Galactic rotation). Arrow uses ~230 km/s as a representative circular speed.
  • Solar apex (motion vs nearby stars / LSR) — Schönrich, Binney & Dehnen (2010): (U,V,W)⊙ = (11.1, 12.24, 7.25) km/s (~18 km/s total), toward Hercules near Vega. doi:10.1111/j.1365-2966.2010.16253.x
  • Vertical oscillation — the Sun is ~20 pc above the midplane and currently moving “up” (W⊙>0). It oscillates through the plane with a period of order ~60–90 Myr.
  • ~1 Myr ahead — at ~230 km/s the Sun travels ~235 pc along its orbit in 1 Myr (1 km/s ≈ 1.023 pc/Myr); a tick marks that approximate future position.

Local Bubble (Local view)

The Sun sits inside a low-density cavity ~300 pc across (radius ~150–165 pc), elongated into a “chimney” perpendicular to the plane. The shell shown is an illustrative ellipsoid (not a fitted mesh of 3D dust voxels). Zucker et al. (2022) find nearby star-forming clouds on the bubble surface; Pelgrims et al. (2020) and Lallement/Vergely 3D dust maps constrain the cavity. Optional surface markers (Taurus, Ophiuchus, Lupus, Chamaeleon, Corona Australis) are approximate.

Dispersed birth cluster

The birth association has dissolved and is spread through the disc (Webb et al. 2020; Portegies Zwart 2009). The translucent ring is a schematic 5–11 kpc band.

Sun’s birth radius

Many estimates place birth near ~5 kpc (now ~8.2 kpc): Ratcliffe et al. (2024) 4.5±0.4 kpc; Baba et al. (2024) ≈5 kpc.
